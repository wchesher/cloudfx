responsible use

What your file is for, and what it is never for

A lumin8r file helps people and AI tools work with you the way you actually work. It is a snapshot of how you described yourself, not a test score, so it must never decide anything about your job.

Use it for

  • Coaching, with a coach you chose
  • Reflecting on how you work
  • Communication support: helping a teammate or lead work with you
  • Rehearsing a situation with your twin
  • Working with your own AI assistant, so it drafts and answers the way you would

Never use it for

  • Hiring, screening candidates, or judging "culture fit"
  • Promotion readiness
  • Performance ratings, or ranking people against each other
  • Pay, discipline or layoffs
  • Predicting someone's performance or whether they will leave
  • Inferring emotions, mental health, or protected characteristics such as age, gender, race, religion or disability

This includes the same request in disguise, or split into small steps that add up to one of these.

The file says no for you

Every file carries a rule for any AI that reads it. If the AI is asked to use the profile for any use on the "never" list, directly, in disguise, or one step at a time, it answers:

"I can't use this profile for that."

The AI does not then help with the request some other way. The rule travels with the file, so it holds in Claude, ChatGPT, or any other assistant you load it into.

Why these limits

  • Self-report is not a measure of job performance. How someone rates themselves on a questionnaire does not tell you how well they do a job, and this file was not built or tested to.
  • Fairness. Answers reflect mood, culture, language and how a person sees themselves on one day. Judging people on them would treat those differences as merit.
  • Workplace privacy and employment rules. Many places regulate personality tests in employment decisions, and in the EU an AI system that infers emotions at work is prohibited. A file that is never used to decide stays clear of both.

Limits, said plainly

  • Small evidence. At most a few workplace choices, two short messages and fifty self-ratings. Read every line as a tendency, not a rule.
  • Self-report. It describes how you see yourself now. It can change, and it is not a fixed type.
  • Middle scores mean nothing. A middle trait score means no clear tendency either way. Do not lean on it.
  • Language background. Some statements ask about vocabulary or wording. Answers to them can reflect your first language or schooling more than your personality, so they carry little weight.
  • Your twin is a simulation. Player two is Claude, an AI. Its answers are guesses from your file, not your words, approval or commitments.

For organizations

  • Employee-held. The employee owns the file and decides who sees which version, for what purpose, and for how long.
  • Voluntary. Do not require it, and do not make it a condition of anything.
  • Declining has no consequence. Not taking the assessment, not sharing, or turning a link off must never count against anyone.
  • Do not keep copies. Read a shared file where it was shared. Do not store it in personnel records or feed it into other systems.
